Five findings

01

The hero spends the whole budget in one screen

All of the page’s warmth, imagery and light live in the hero’s right column. Scroll once and the material story ends: nothing warm or pictorial recurs until the CTA, four sections later. The following two-and-a-half viewports are flat ink and type, so the hero reads as a promise the page doesn’t keep. This, more than any single seam, is the “not premium” feeling — luxury is continuity of material.

FixLet the hero’s afterglow decay through the strip and the belief — SectionGlow’s amber tail up from 0.11 to ~0.16 at this seam only, taller, dying out before the light act. One continuous scene instead of hero + black void.

02

Three moments, one volume

H1 at display scale, then the fields ribbon at 80px full-bleed caps, then the belief at display scale again — three headline-sized statements stacked with nothing between them. When everything is a moment, nothing is. The ribbon is the offender: it’s meta content (a list of client fields) dressed as a headline, and it’s also the only thing on the page that moves unbidden — right at the handover, it outshouts both the H1 above it and the belief below it.

FixDemote it to a credit strip: “Who we work with” kicker + 12px mono caps between hairlines, marquee kept but quiet, tight under the hero as its colophon. The belief becomes the undisputed second moment.

03

The void between ribbon and index

224px of padding above the belief, 160px below, and the statement rests at 15% opacity until the scroll-fill lights it — with the fill only completing as the track nears the top of the viewport. Mid-scroll, the frame is roughly three-quarters empty ink, and a fast scroller passes the statement before it ever finishes lighting. The screenshot compresses this to nothing; the browser makes you live in it for two screens.

FixHalve the air (pt-56 → pt-28), raise the resting fill 0.15 → 0.35, finish the fill by mid-viewport. The statement is always legible as a mass; the fill stays as the event.

A mid-scroll frame, to scale

04

The cut to bone has no arrival

The dark-to-light razor cut is legitimate in this system — but here the bone act opens with ~190px of empty paper, then a hairline floating mid-air, then a heading that belongs to a row, not to the act. After a hero whose whole seam philosophy is “the two sides must read as one”, the loudest boundary on the page is the one with no design in it. A cut this hard needs the new chapter to start speaking immediately, or it reads like a template change.

FixOpen the light act with structure inside the first 120px: “What we do” kicker + a full-width rule, then the rows. The cut becomes a deliberate page-turn.

05

The FAQ obeys the wrong era’s rule

Your own FaqSection contract: cream for pages with a light middle act, dark for pages that run dark hero-to-close. This page has a light middle and a dark FAQ — decisions from two different versions of the page cohabiting. That’s where the zebra comes from: dark, light, dark, light, dark. Restlessness reads as indecision, and indecision never reads premium.

Fixtone="cream" here — the deeper ivory (#DCD3C3) steps the light act down instead of cutting out of it, and the dark close (the warm-card CTA + footer) finishes the page as the hero’s bookend.

Five stripes vs three acts

The seam rules

One grammar, applied everywhere:

— Dark into dark: both sides resolve to the anchor tone (#14100B); the wash decays it to flat ink. One colour on both sides of the line means no line.

— Dark into light: always a deliberate cut — and the light side leads with structure (kicker + rule) inside its first 120px.

— Never two devices at one boundary: a glow handover or a page-turn, decided per seam, never both half-happening.
